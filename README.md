## Getting Started

The Galvanometer GalleryDescription:The Galvanometer Gallery is a mobile application for collectors of early electrical physics apparatus, historians of laboratory science, and curators of 19th-century measurement technology, dedicated to cataloging the sensitive electromagnetic instruments used to detect and measure electric current before the era of digital multimeters and solid-state sensors.Systematize your collection of the first windows into the world of electrons—from mirror galvanometers that used light beams to track tiny currents to heavy brass tangent galvanometers used to measure the Earth's magnetic field, from astatic needle pairs to "dead-beat" moving coil movements, from early Wheatstone bridges to gold-leaf electroscopes.Create a digital archive of scientific discovery by recording the movement type, coil resistance, sensitivity, era, and the laboratory or university history of each specimen.Example Object Fields (The "Electrical Apparatus" Model):Laboratory Identifier: Unique code (e.g., "TGG-SIEMENS-1888-BERLIN-102")Instrument Type: Category (mirror galvanometer, tangent galvanometer, ballistic galvanometer, moving coil meter, electroscope, Wheatstone bridge).Manufacturer and Maker: Brand or instrument maker (Siemens & Halske, Elliott Bros, Leeds & Northrup, Weston Electrical Instrument Co., Nalder Bros).Country of Origin: Where it was manufactured (Germany, England, USA, France).Era of Production: Decade or specific year (1860s, 1880s, 1910s, 1930s).Operating Principle: Electromagnetic (moving coil, moving magnet), electrostatic, or thermal (hot wire).Internal Resistance: Coil resistance measured in Ohms ($\Omega$).Sensitivity and Scale: Scale divisions, deflection per microampere, or focal length for mirror types.Materials and Housing: Primary construction (mahogany base, brass leveling feet, glass bell jar, silk-insulated copper wire).Dimensions and Weight: Physical size and mass.Condition State: Coil continuity, suspension fiber integrity (intact/broken), original lacquer, calibration status.Included Accessories: Original wooden travel case, shunt resistors, leveling screws, external mirrors, or calibration charts.Markings and Engravings: Serial numbers, university property stamps, patent dates, or maker’s signatures.Provenance: Where it was found (university physics lab, decommissioned telegraph station, research institute, scientific auction).
